MOVE 1 · A Conversion

3D model → structural analysis model

A 3D model is drawn for looking at. An analysis model is built for calculating with. Converting one into the other is the step every competitor glosses over, and it is where GeoSoft's existing strength gives us the advantage.

What the software does automatically

  • Reads the client's model: IFC, SACS, STAAD or a scan

MOVE 1 · C Loading

Applying the loads

Some loads the software works out for itself. Some come from the client's records and are entered by our engineer. The environmental loads are generated from the site's metocean criteria.

AUTOMATIC

Self-weight of the whole structure, computed member by member from section, length and density — with allowances for welds, fittings and coatings kept separate and visible.

ENGINEER ENTERS

Equipment and piping loads from the client's design documents. Where equipment has been changed since design, the new weight and position are entered and the difference is immediately visible.

  • Equipment weight and centre of gravity
  • Piping dry, operating and hydrotest weights
  • Pipe-stress support reactions, imported directly
  • Live and laydown areas by deck zone

GENERATED

Environmental loads from the site criteria:

  • Wave loading on every submerged member
  • Current, combined with the wave kinematics
  • Wind on the topsides and exposed steel
  • Marine growth on diameter and weight
  • Seismic, as response spectrum or time history
Load combinationsEvery combination records its code, edition, clause and factors, so any result can be traced back to the rule that produced it.

BE HONEST ABOUT THIS

A full finite element analysis of a real platform is not fast, and we should never suggest otherwise:

  • Small structure: days to weeks
  • Typical jacket: three weeks to a month
  • Large or complex jacket: two to three months

Most of that is not computing time. It is building and checking the model, chasing missing data, agreeing assumptions, and verifying results — which is exactly the engineering a client is paying for.

And this is precisely why move 2 mattersHaving paid for that analysis once, the client should not have to wait weeks again every time a load changes. That is the problem the reduced-order model solves.

The reduced model holds the structure only. Loads are computed fresh each time and fed into it. Nothing is re-opened, nothing is re-analysed. The only thing that invalidates it is a change to the steel itself — and then it is re-extracted in seconds.

MOVE 2 · TECHNOLOGY Method selection

How we build the reduced model

This family of methods is called reduced-basis finite element analysis. Several techniques exist. We have chosen the two that suit our models and our timescale, and deliberately deferred the rest.

BUILD NOW

  1. Modal truncationKeep the structure's dominant mode shapes. Simple, fast to extract, and sufficient for global response, sway, frequency tracking and live monitoring. This is what our prototype already does.
  2. Craig-Bampton reductionKeep chosen points exactly — sensor locations, fatigue-critical joints, pile heads — and reduce only the interior. This gives accurate stress where we need it, which modal truncation alone does not. Essential for fatigue.

LATER, AND ONLY IF NEEDED

  1. Proper orthogonal decomposition (POD)Builds the basis from snapshots of many solved cases.
  2. Reduced basis with greedy trainingSpans a family of different structures, not just one.
  3. Balanced truncationFrom control theory; keeps what is both controllable and observable.
  4. Proper generalised decomposition (PGD)Builds the solution as a product of separate parameter functions.

Why deferred: these methods earn their complexity when the model must learn across many structural variations — hundreds of training analyses, a far larger build. We do not have that requirement today. Modal truncation and Craig-Bampton give us the capability we are actually selling.

One capability we will add early regardlessAn accuracy indicator on every reduced solve, measuring how well the reduced answer satisfies the full equations. It means the fast model can state its own reliability rather than asking to be trusted — which is what a regulator or class society will expect.
